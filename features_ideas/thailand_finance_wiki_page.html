Thailand company finances — one-page guide

CA · bénéfice · expenses · VAT · impôts · companies vs branches

The core formula
Chiffre d'affaires (CA) − Expenses (charges) = Bénéfice (profit)

Everything else on this page is a detail of one of those three things.

The 7 concepts
CA — Chiffre d'affaires

Total revenue from sales, before anything is subtracted. The "top line." Does NOT include VAT — VAT collected from customers is not yours, it's just passing through.

Bénéfice (profit)

What's left after all expenses are paid. The "bottom line." This is what gets taxed via impôts sur les sociétés. Can be distributed to owners or reinvested.

Expenses (charges)

Everything the company spends to operate: salaries, rent, suppliers, equipment, etc. you control this — reducing legitimate expenses reduces profit and therefore tax. Keep receipts (net amount, without VAT) to justify each expense.

Impôts sur les sociétés

Corporate income tax. Paid once a year (or in instalments). Calculated on bénéfice only — not on CA. In Thailand ~20% of net profit. you cannot avoid this — only reduce it by reducing bénéfice.

VAT collected (TVA collectée) VAT paid (TVA déductible)

When you sell, you add 7% VAT to your invoice — that money belongs to the government, you collect it on their behalf. When you buy from suppliers, you pay VAT on purchases. The difference is what you actually owe the government, filed monthly or quarterly.

VAT to pay = VAT collected from customers − VAT paid to suppliers

This is why supplier receipts WITH VAT matter — they reduce your VAT bill.

The VAT flow on a sale
You sell at 1,000 THB (net) → Invoice shows 1,070 THB → CA = 1,000 + 70 THB goes to gov.

The 70 THB is never profit. Your CA is always the net (ex-VAT) amount.

What to keep and why
Invoices you issue (with VAT)

Proof of your CA. Required for VAT declaration. Keep all — they define your revenue and VAT collected.

Receipts from suppliers (with VAT)

Lets you deduct the VAT you paid → reduces your monthly VAT bill. Also proves the expense is real for income tax.

Receipts from suppliers (without VAT)

Some suppliers are VAT-exempt (small traders, etc). Still valid to justify the expense for income tax — just no VAT to deduct.

What to discard

Personal expenses with no business purpose. Receipts for things unrelated to the company's activity. These don't reduce your tax anyway.

3 companies + branches — what is linked, what isn't
Each company is a separate legal entity

CA, expenses, bénéfice, VAT, and impôts are all calculated and filed per company. You cannot mix revenues or expenses between Company A and Company B. Each has its own accounting, its own VAT number, its own tax return.

Branches are part of the company

A branch (สาขา) is NOT a separate legal entity. It shares the VAT number and tax number of the parent company. Its revenues and expenses are consolidated into the parent company's accounts. You track branches separately for management, but legally and fiscally it's one entity.

Practical consequence: If Company A and Company B sell to each other, that's a real taxable transaction for both sides. If Branch 1 and Branch 2 (same company) transact, it's an internal transfer — not a sale, no VAT, no CA.
