01 — Brand Identity

Who we are

Capybara Coffee was born from a simple question: what if the most memorable part of your visit wasn't the drink? Across Thailand's most beloved destinations, we build spaces where capybaras, meerkats, and their friends are the stars.

Our guests don't just visit a café — they slow down, connect with animals, and leave with a story. We are an experience brand first, a coffee brand second.

Mission

To create unforgettable moments of joy through the gentle connection between humans and animals.

Brand Personality

Five values that define every decision — from a button label to a social post to the colour of a cup sleeve.

Warm

Every interaction — visual or verbal — should feel like a welcome, not a transaction.

→ Cream backgrounds, amber accents, friendly copy

Memorable

We exist to create stories people tell. Design reinforces this — it doesn't compete with it.

→ Strong visuals, no visual clutter, bold moments

Genuine

No hype, no hollow superlatives. We say what's true and let the experience speak.

→ "30 minutes" not "an unforgettable time"

Curious

We share what we know about our animals. We invite guests to learn, not just look.

→ Pedagogical signage, educational captions

Caring

Animal welfare is non-negotiable. This shows in how we write, design, and photograph.

→ Never show animal distress, always show space & calm
01

02

02 — Logo System

The mark

Our logo is a circular badge mark featuring a capybara sitting in a coffee cup. It is warm, recognisable, and deliberately charming. The v2 mark (no outer outline) is the current standard.

Clear space

Always maintain a minimum clear space equal to ¼ of the logo's diameter on all sides. No text, graphic element, or edge of a surface should enter this zone.

Minimum sizes

Digital

  • Minimum 32px diameter for UI use (favicon, app icon)
  • Minimum 80px for body / inline use
  • No upper size limit

Print & physical

  • Minimum 15mm diameter for print
  • Minimum 20mm for embroidery / emboss
  • Use vector source file for print above 50mm

✓ Do

  • Use on Cream, Espresso, or plain white backgrounds
  • Use the v2 mark (no outline) as the default everywhere
  • Maintain the original proportions at all times
  • Use on solid colour backgrounds only
  • Ensure sufficient contrast at all sizes

✗ Never

  • Stretch, squash, or rotate the mark
  • Place the logo on a busy photograph without a solid backing
  • Change any colours in the illustration
  • Add drop shadows, gradients, or effects
  • Use the v1 outline logo (retired — do not use)
  • Recreate the logo in any other typeface

03 — Color System

The palette

Four colors. Every color has a precise role. Nothing outside this palette should appear in official brand materials.

Cream

#F7F2E9

Primary background · Page base · Large fields

Espresso

#2F2823

Primary text · Dark sections · Footer · Nav

Amber

#B9854E

Headings · Brand warmth · Section labels · CTA borders

Sage

#7C9A4D

Accent only · CTAs · Badges · Highlights · Nature reference

Color roles by volume

Approximate coverage across a typical page or document.

Cream — backgrounds ~60%
Espresso — text & dark areas ~25%
Amber — warmth & structure ~12%
Sage — accents only ~3%

Color pairing rules

Cream on Espresso only. Never on Amber or Sage backgrounds.
Espresso on Cream or White. Readable at all sizes.
Amber on Cream or Espresso. For labels, borders, dividers.
Sage on Cream or Espresso. CTA fills, badges. Sparingly.
White on Espresso sections only (body copy, captions).

Forbidden color combinations

✗ Sage on Amber (or reverse)

Sage text → illegible Amber text

Neither colour reads clearly on the other. Contrast ratio fails accessibility minimums.

✗ Amber as body text

This paragraph is written in Amber. At reading size it becomes strained and loses all contrast against cream — notice how your eye struggles to track the line.

Amber is for short labels and headings only. Never body copy.

✗ Sage as a page background

Section heading Body text on sage background. It feels like a nature website, not a warm coffee experience. The hierarchy breaks — sage disappears as a CTA accent.

Sage backgrounds overpower the accent role. Stick to Cream or Espresso for large fields.

✗ Colours outside the system

Custom orange Template blue Canva purple ← None of these.

No tints, gradients, or "close enough" swaps. Four colours, always.

05 — Voice & Tone

How we speak

Our voice is consistent — it belongs to the brand. Our tone shifts slightly by context (warmer on social, more informative on signage) but the character never changes: genuine, warm, and specific.

01
Specific,
not vague

Say "30 minutes with our capybaras" — not "an unforgettable experience." Concrete details build trust.

02
Warm,
not gushing

One honest sentence beats three excited ones. We trust the experience to speak for itself.

03
Knowledgeable,
not academic

We share what we know about our animals in plain, interesting sentences — not Wikipedia prose.

04
Inviting,
not pushy

No countdown timers, no "limited spots." We invite — we don't pressure. The experience earns the visit.

✓ Write this

Come and spend 30 minutes with our capybaras. Feed them, sit beside them, and let the day slow down.
Specific time, real action, emotional benefit — no hype.
Our animals are cared for daily by trained staff. Their wellbeing comes first — always.
Direct, confidence-building, no hollow promises.
400 THB · Adults · 30 minutes · One drink required.
Pricing: clear and honest. No asterisks, no surprises.

✗ Not this

Experience the MOST AMAZING adventure of your life!!!! 🐾🐾🐾
Hyperbole and excess punctuation undermine credibility.
We are passionate about providing world-class animal interactions in a unique environment.
Generic brand-speak. Says nothing. Replace with facts.
LIMITED SPOTS — BOOK NOW BEFORE IT'S TOO LATE
Pressure tactics are off-brand. We invite, we don't chase.

Tone by channel

🌐

Warm and informative. Lead with the experience, follow with logistics. Visitors need to know what to expect, how to get there, and what it costs — make it effortless.

"Located in Samui, Phangan, Bangkok & Chiang Mai — each with its own capybaras."
📷

Instagram

Playful, intimate, real. Share moments, not promotions. Let the photo carry the emotion — the caption adds context or a quiet smile.

"She came for the coffee. She stayed for him." ↗
🪧

In-store

Clear and brief. Guests are already here — guide them, inform them, occasionally delight them. One idea per sign. Never sell to someone who already bought.

"Feeding time: hold the snack flat, palm open."
🖨

Print & Marketing

Lead with the most compelling fact. Follow with location, price, CTA. Short sentences. No filler adjectives.

06 — Photography & Imagery

What our world looks like

Photography is our strongest brand asset — it shows what no tagline can. Every image should feel warm, natural, and real. Never staged. Never stock.

☀

Warm light

Golden hour, natural daylight. Avoid harsh overhead flash. The space is warm — photos should feel it.

🌿

Nature-forward

Show plants, textures, outdoor spaces. Reinforce the nature theme — not just the animals.

♡

Genuine moments

Guests and animals in real interaction. Candid over posed. Reaction shots over product shots.

✕

Never: Stock photos

No generic café or animal photos. Every image must be taken at one of our locations.

✕

Never: Heavy filters

No heavy Instagram filters, desaturation, or dramatic edits. Light colour grading only — preserve warmth.

✕

Never: Animal distress

Never publish images that could suggest forced interaction, stress, or poor animal welfare. This is non-negotiable.

Image composition rules

✓ Prioritise

  • Close-up texture shots of animals (fur, expressions)
  • Hands feeding or gently touching animals
  • Children and families in natural moments
  • The coffee and drinks as secondary elements (in hand, on table beside animal)
  • Lush greenery and outdoor ambiance shots
  • Staff with animals — shows care and relationship

✗ Avoid

  • Animals isolated from human presence (feels zoo-like)
  • Cluttered backgrounds or messy visible infrastructure
  • Heavy text overlaid directly on photos
  • Promotional graphics (discount %, QR codes) over photos
  • Dark, underexposed, or blurry images on official channels

12 — Reference

Non-Negotiables

Eight rules. If any one of these is broken, the brand breaks. No exceptions, no "just this once."

✗

Unauthorised fonts

Only Cabinet Grotesk, Next Southerland Serif, Next Southerland Script, and Satoshi. Nothing from Canva, Google Fonts, or templates.

The previous website used a script font for all headings. It is now retired.
✗

Sage as a heading colour

Sage is an accent only — CTAs, badges, small highlights. Headings are Espresso or Amber. Never Sage.

The current website uses Sage green on nearly every heading. This is retired.
✗

Espresso sections used heavily

Dark backgrounds are for the hero, footer, and one or two feature blocks. Not every other section.

Alternating cream/espresso every block makes the page feel heavy and dated.
✗

Promotional overlays on photos

"20% OFF" banners, QR codes, and discount graphics placed over brand photography reduce perceived quality instantly.

Promotions live in dedicated sections — never embedded in images.
✗

Colours outside the palette

Four colours, fixed. No tints, no gradients, no "close enough." Override any template default that deviates.

Orange, blue, purple, grey — none of these exist in the brand.
✗

Text directly over photographs

Text always sits on a solid colour surface. Never raw over a photo — not even with opacity tricks.

Use a Cream or Espresso band. The photo goes beside the text, not behind it.
✗

Logo v1 in active use

The v1 mark (outline ring, "Healthy Drinks & Coffee Bar") is retired. v2 everywhere — website, print, social, merch.

If you find v1 in use anywhere, replace it immediately.
✗

Hype language in copy

No "AMAZING!", no "BOOK NOW BEFORE IT'S TOO LATE", no excessive punctuation. We invite — we don't pressure.

"An unforgettable experience!!!" → "30 minutes with our capybaras."
